# Source Register

Verified: 2026-08-15

## 1. Oral History Best Practices

- Organization: Oral History Association
- URL: https://oralhistory.org/best-practices/
- Used for: preparation, documented informed consent, pre-interview discussion, open-ended question guide, recording quality, narrator expectations, preservation and access planning
- Boundary: professional oral-history guidance is adapted proportionately for a private family conversation; the article does not claim that a family recording is an institutional oral-history project

## 2. OHA Statement on Ethics

- Organization: Oral History Association
- URL: https://oralhistory.org/oha-statement-on-ethics/
- Used for: dignity, privacy, right not to answer, review before public use, restriction/redaction choices, transparent intended use, contextual use of excerpts
- Boundary: the article gives general ethical guidance, not legal advice or a universal release form

## 3. For Participants in Oral History Interviews

- Organization: Oral History Association
- URL: https://oralhistory.org/for-participants-in-oral-history-interviews/
- Used for: participant rights, breaks, stopping, intended use, access restrictions, withdrawal limits once material becomes public
- Boundary: public archives can involve legal and copyright considerations beyond a private family recording; readers are told to seek qualified advice for public or institutional projects

## 4. Preserving Family Stories

- Organization: Library of Congress
- URL: https://www.loc.gov/static/portals/families/documents/PreservingFamilyStories.pdf
- Used for: short open questions, avoiding yes/no questions, testing equipment, selecting a quiet location, recording date/place/names/topic, listening without interruption, taking notes, pre-interview value
- Boundary: question wording in the article is independently written; the Library's list is not reproduced

## 5. How to Do Oral History

- Organization: Smithsonian Institution Archives
- URL: https://siarchives.si.edu/sites/default/files/pdfs/How_to_Do_Oral_History_Guide_2020.pdf
- Used for: first-person focus, quiet-room preparation, permission to record, identifying the interview at the start, descriptive file naming, copying recordings to separate storage, optional uncompressed preservation audio
- Boundary: technical suggestions are presented as options, not requirements for a meaningful family conversation
