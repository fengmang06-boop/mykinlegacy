## Content promise

Provide 40 adaptable, open-ended questions plus a complete before-during-after workflow. The guide must be useful without purchasing anything and must treat consent, privacy, narrator review, context, and preservation as core steps rather than fine print.

## Observed SERP gap

The 2026-08-15 search snapshot showed many pages organized mainly around large prompt counts (25, 50, 75, 100, or more questions). Some mention recording permission, but fewer make informed consent, intended access, the narrator's right not to answer, post-interview review, metadata, and preservation part of one practical family workflow.

No absolute search-volume claim is made. The candidate is justified by topical fit and an observable content-quality gap, not by invented demand estimates.

## Intent boundary

This page owns the interview workflow: choosing a focus, agreeing on use, asking open questions, listening, handling sensitive material, labeling the recording, and giving the narrator a review path.

It must not compete with:

- `/journal/how-to-create-a-family-keepsake`, which owns turning selected evidence into a keepsake;
- local candidate `/journal/how-to-preserve-family-history`, which owns physical and digital archive preservation;
- `/journal/what-is-a-family-crest`, which owns crest, coat-of-arms, and heraldry clarification;
- `/family-legacy-collection`, which owns the commercial Complete Collection offer.

## Editorial requirements

- Useful without purchase.
- Do not imply that memory alone proves genealogy, ancestry, dates, or legal facts.
- Attribute recollections and preserve uncertainty.
- Ask permission before recording.
- Explain intended use, audience, storage, and review before recording.
- Respect the narrator's right to skip a question, pause, end, restrict, or decline publication.
- Do not use urgency based on death, illness, age, regret, or fear.
- Do not reproduce long question lists from any source.
- Cite primary institutional guidance from the Oral History Association, Library of Congress, and Smithsonian Institution Archives.
- Keep the commercial transition short and at the end.

## Proposed structure

1. Give the short answer and a 10-question starter set.
2. Agree on purpose, recording, audience, and boundaries.
3. Choose one interview focus.
4. Use 40 questions across five topic groups.
5. Apply neutral follow-up prompts.
6. Handle sensitive stories and conflicting memories carefully.
7. Record cleanly without turning the conversation into a production.
8. Label, review, preserve, and control access after the interview.
9. Separate memory, documented fact, and later interpretation.
10. Offer a concise next step into keepsake creation.
